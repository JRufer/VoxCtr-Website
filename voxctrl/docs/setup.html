GETTING STARTED / SETUP WIZARD

Setup wizard

New in v0.4.0. On a machine with no config, voxctrl opens a seven-step wizard rather than a settings window full of defaults nobody chose. It picks a transcription engine and model, binds a hotkey and registers it with your desktop, chooses an overlay, runs a live dictation test, and optionally adds a voice.

ⓘ The wizard deliberately offers a subset of what Settings exposes. A first launch is the wrong moment to choose between eleven Whisper variants and every Piper voice. Everything omitted here is still reachable in Settings afterwards.

Opening it again

  • voxctrl --setup — the aliases --wizard, --setup-wizard and --first-run do the same thing. Running it while voxctrl is already going reopens the wizard in the running instance rather than starting a second copy.
  • Settings → General → Open setup wizard.

Your current settings stay in place until you change them in the wizard, so reopening it is safe.

Every choice is saved as you make it

Each step writes straight to ~/.config/voxctrl/ the moment you pick something — there is no "apply" at the end. Quitting halfway keeps everything you chose up to that point, and skipping the wizard entirely leaves you with working defaults.

The seven steps

1 · Welcome

What the next few minutes cover, and a reminder that nothing here is permanent.

2 · Engine

whisper.cpp or Moonshine, and a model size inside each. The card for each engine shows relative speed, quiet-room accuracy, and how much of that accuracy survives a noisy room — whisper.cpp is the more accurate in a quiet space and scales further; Moonshine holds up better against fans, keyboards and traffic, and stays fast on any CPU.

Engine Sizes offered GPU
whisper.cpp tiny 75 MB · base 142 MB · small 466 MB · medium 1.5 GB · large-v3 3.1 GB Yes — CUDA or Vulkan
Moonshine tiny 100 MB · base 250 MB No — CPU-native

A single GPU offloading switch sets engine.whisper_cpp.device to auto (on) or cpu (off); auto hands the work to whichever GPU path the build has. Moonshine is unaffected by it. The model you choose is downloaded before the wizard lets you continue, and a model already on disk is reported as such.

3 · Hotkey

Pick one of four gestures, then record the keys. The binding is handed to the XDG GlobalShortcuts portal there and then, so you find out on this step — not later — whether your desktop accepted it.

Gesture Behaviour
Tap to talk (toggle) Press once to start, press again to stop. Hands stay free while you speak.
Double-tap to talk (double_tap) Two quick presses start dictation; one press stops. Hard to trigger by accident.
Hold to talk (hold) Hold the keys while speaking, release to stop. Like a walkie-talkie.
Double-tap & hold (double_tap_hold) Tap once, then press and hold. Release to stop. Never collides with a plain shortcut.

See Hotkeys for the timing fields and what happens when a desktop has no shortcuts portal.

4 · Overlay

Whether to show anything while the mic is live, which of the eight built-in styles, and where on screen it sits (top, center or bottom). Choosing a style also turns the overlay on. Picking No overlay leaves only the tray icon as feedback. Custom overlays are not offered here — a machine on its first launch has none. See Overlays.

5 · Test

A real text box and your real binding: click in, trigger the gesture you just bound, speak, and watch the transcript land. This is the step that catches a hotkey the desktop silently refused, a muted microphone, or a model that did not finish downloading. You can continue past it and come back from Settings → Hotkeys.

6 · Voice (optional)

Whether voxctrl should speak replies aloud, and with which engine. Each card plays a sample in place before you commit to a download.

Engine Kind Download
Breeze-TTS-2 neural · expressive ~1.2 GB · gated
Pocket TTS neural · voice cloning ~500 MB · gated
Piper TTS onnx · ~11 voices ~60 MB per voice
Inflect Micro compact neural 38 MB
eSpeak-NG formant synth bundled — nothing to fetch

Gated means HuggingFace only serves the weights to an account that has accepted the licence. Create a token at huggingface.co/settings/tokens, accept the licence at huggingface.co/BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2 and huggingface.co/kyutai/pocket-tts, then paste the token into the wizard — one token covers both, and it is saved where Settings → TTS keeps it. Exporting HF_TOKEN works instead and takes precedence over a saved token: it is shown in the UI but kept out of your config file. Skipping this step leaves speech output off; Settings → TTS turns it on later. See Voice output.

7 · Done

A summary of every choice, a pointer to the tray icon, and a map of which Settings tab holds what — including the things the wizard skipped on purpose: Output Commands, per-binding LLM cleanup, audio devices and the MCP server.

Updating

Also new in v0.4.0: voxctrl checks GitHub for a newer release shortly after launch, shows what changed, and — if you say yes — downloads the build matching your installation (CPU AppImage, Vulkan AppImage or Windows installer), verifies it against the checksum GitHub published, replaces itself and restarts.

  • Nothing is replaced until a complete, verified file is on disk, so a failed update leaves the working version alone.
  • The check is one unauthenticated GET to the public release listing, carrying no identifier of you or your machine. It is the only network request voxctrl makes on its own.
  • Settings → General turns it off. With it off, voxctrl contacts GitHub only when you press Check for updates.
